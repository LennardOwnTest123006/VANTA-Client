Release of VANTA Client for Minecraft Java Edition 1.21.11 (Fabric Loader 0.19.5, Fabric API 0.141.6+1.21.11, Java 21). It fixes the frame-rate caps that the presets and profiles of 1.1.0 wrote (the cause of "exactly 30 FPS after choosing Low"), adds the Max FPS preset and a one-click Boost FPS, keeps every button clickable in small windows, cuts VANTA's own drawing work per frame, and puts the Performance pack mods whose licences allow it into the mods bundle, so a manual installation gets Sodium, Lithium, FerriteCore, ImmediatelyFast and Iris without a download; EntityCulling is offered in game with one click.

## Added

- **Max FPS** preset: Fast graphics, 5 chunks render and simulation distance, minimal particles, clouds off, smooth lighting and entity shadows off, entity distance 50 %, biome blend off, no mipmaps and the menu background blur off. It is the first tab of the Performance Center's preset card, next to Low, Balanced, High and Ultra
- **Boost FPS**, the primary button of the preset card (also in the command palette): applies Max FPS, sets the frame-rate limit to Unlimited and VSync off, switches the VANTA menu to a solid background without particles and installs the Performance pack when a member is neither loaded nor installed. Only that click downloads anything; when the pack is present, or the Modrinth integration is not available, nothing is downloaded. The notification says when a restart is needed
- **Performance pack in the mods bundle**: `vanta-client-1.2.0-mods.zip` carries the newest Minecraft 1.21.11 Fabric build of Sodium, Lithium, FerriteCore, ImmediatelyFast and Iris next to the VANTA jar and Fabric API, unmodified as published on Modrinth and verified by size and SHA-512 when the release is built. The zip also holds `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt` (every licence text, with the file it covers; Sodium's PolyForm Shield 1.0.0 terms are passed on), `PERFORMANCE-PACK.txt` (the versions in this zip, also in the release notes) and `performance-pack.json`. `INSTALL.txt` lists the pack jars, says that Iris and Sodium belong together and warns about duplicate copies of a mod. No mod version is fixed in VANTA: the release workflow resolves them on Modrinth when it builds the release and fails rather than ship without a licence text
- **EntityCulling stays a download**: its licence does not allow redistribution, so it is not in the zip. The *Performance pack* card of Mods & Shaders installs it with one click
- **One-time offer at start**: a game that the VANTA Launcher did not start asks once per game start, at the main menu, *Boost your FPS?*, when Performance pack members are missing. *Install* records the pack jars that came with the bundle in `config/vanta/modrinth.json` (so the Installed tab can disable and remove them) and installs the missing members from Modrinth; *Not now* turns the offer off for good (*Settings → Performance → Offer the Performance pack at start* switches it back on). Nothing is downloaded without that click

## Improved

- Presets only tune render work and never set *Max framerate* or *VSync*; the frame-rate limit is set only by the frame-rate limit chooser, by Boost FPS and by a profile's own frame-rate choice. The preset descriptions no longer mention a cap, and the built-in *Performance* profile uses Max FPS with the limit unlimited
- The interface never shrinks below Minecraft's 320 x 240: the UI scale (UI scale times large text) is lowered, never below 1, so that the logical screen is always at least 320 x 240 px, and layout, drawing and input use the same clamped scale
- Less drawing work per frame, with pixel-identical output: horizontal gradients (the main menu's horizon, vignette and PLAY button) are one native gradient each instead of one fill per column; icons are rasterised once per size and replayed as rectangles (fills per icon at 16 px down from 19 to 11 on average); text widths and styled text components are cached; a clipped label is ellipsized by binary search (65 to 9 width measurements for a typical Modrinth description); the HUD reads only what enabled widgets show (25 to 15 game lookups per tick for the default layout); the crosshair samples inputs once per tick and caches its geometry; the CPU reading is reused for 250 ms; frame times are sampled once per frame
- Every screen is checked at 19 window sizes, at scale 1 and with large text, for buttons that cannot be clicked or that overlap each other, and the Mods & Shaders screen in 15 states at every size. The headless game test measures VANTA's frame cost in the real game and clicks the Mods & Shaders buttons in a windowed game the way a mouse does

## Fixed

- **Exactly 30 FPS, or a 60 / 120 FPS cap, after choosing a preset or profile in 1.1.0.** The Low preset wrote a 60 FPS cap and Balanced a 120 FPS cap into Minecraft's *Max framerate* option, and the built-in profiles carried those caps along: *Performance* (Low) a 60 FPS cap, *Default* (Balanced) a 120 FPS cap. A player who picked *Low* or the *Performance* profile to get more frames was capped and, with a driver-forced 60 Hz VSync on top of Minecraft's limiter, could land at exactly 30 FPS. Presets and profiles no longer write a cap; every built-in profile derives its vanilla limit and VSync from its own frame-rate choice. A cap that 1.1.0 already wrote stays in `options.txt` until you choose *Unlimited* in the frame-rate limit chooser (or press *Boost FPS*) once
- The *Frame rate limit* row in Settings → Performance now really writes the vanilla Max framerate and VSync options when it is changed there or by a profile; before, only the Performance Center's own chooser did. Loading the settings at start never writes `options.txt`
- In a small game window (854 x 480 at GUI scale 2, the 320 x 240 minimum, or with large text) several buttons were drawn but could not be clicked because their panel had run out of room: *Install*, *View on Modrinth*, *Disable*, *Remove* and the other actions of the Mods & Shaders detail panel, *Quit Game* and the quick-access row of the main menu at 640 x 360, *Vanilla options* in Settings and the HUD editor's grid and snap switches. The detail text now scrolls above a footer that keeps the actions on screen, the main menu picks an arrangement that fits the window, the Settings footer wraps, the HUD editor toolbar wraps onto two lines and the settings category rail scrolls when the window is too short
- Profiles written by 1.1.0 or earlier no longer re-apply a 60 / 120 FPS cap on an upgraded install: on the first start, built-in profiles you never changed are refreshed from the current defaults (profile files move to schema version 2; renamed or edited built-ins and your own profiles keep their content), and activating any profile derives the vanilla frame-rate limit and VSync from the profile's own frame-rate choice. *Reset all settings* now ends at *Unlimited* with VSync off. A 1.1.0 client ignores schema-2 profile files and recreates its built-ins if you downgrade
- *Boost FPS* and the *Boost your FPS?* offer install only the Performance pack members that are really missing; a member Fabric already loaded from a jar Modrinth does not recognise is never downloaded twice, not even as Iris' dependency. While a Modrinth download is still running, *Boost FPS* applies its settings, queues no second pack install and shows *Download still running*; the Performance Center's button waits until the install is done, and one *Boost applied* toast follows per click
- A pack jar you switched off by hand (`.jar.disabled`), or one built for another Minecraft version, is no longer taken over into VANTA's index; *Install* then switches an identical disabled copy back on and says so, or downloads the current version next to it
- The Mods & Shaders restart banner's *Restart* / *Quit* button waits until a running download has landed, so quitting can no longer abandon a half-finished install
- Escape (or any other way of closing) the *Boost your FPS?* dialog counts as *Not now*, and the dialog says so
- Cosmetics cards no longer overlap the section below or sit beyond the end of the list after switching sections in a small window; layouts that need a second pass now get it in the same frame
- Confirmation, profile-name, import and save-preset dialogs follow the window after a resize, a fullscreen toggle or a UI-scale change instead of sitting off-screen while blocking every click; dropdown lists and the colour palette stay on the screen on short windows
- Ctrl+F and other screen shortcuts no longer steal the keyboard from an open dialog or dropdown; Enter keeps activating the dialog's default button

## Notes

- Manual installation: copy **all** jars from the `mods/` folder of `vanta-client-1.2.0-mods.zip` into your `mods` folder and delete older copies of Sodium, Iris, Lithium, FerriteCore and ImmediatelyFast first; Fabric refuses to start with two copies of one mod, and Iris requires exactly the Sodium build in the zip. EntityCulling is not in the zip: accept the *Boost your FPS?* offer or install it on the *Performance pack* card. The VANTA Launcher keeps installing the whole pack from Modrinth as before
- The pack mods in the zip are third-party projects by their own authors under their own licences (Sodium: PolyForm Shield 1.0.0; the texts are in `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt`), shipped unmodified; VANTA Client itself stays MIT. Which versions a release holds is printed in its release notes and in `PERFORMANCE-PACK.txt`
- What VANTA itself costs is measured in the CI game test (software renderer, no GPU): the test fails when the VANTA HUD averages more than 4 ms per frame, and it passes; the measured values are written to `vanta-perf-probe.json` in every run. The 30 FPS reports are explained by the caps above, not by the HUD. How much faster the game runs without the caps, or with the Performance pack, on a real graphics card was not measured; VANTA promises no number
- Nothing new is sent anywhere. The offer at start and Boost FPS contact Modrinth only after your click, like an *Install* on the Mods & Shaders screen
- VANTA is a legitimate client: it contains no cheats, no combat automation, no packet manipulation and no anti-cheat bypasses
- Every file is published by the release workflow with its SHA-256 in the release manifest and in `SHA256SUMS.txt`; compare it before installing. Nothing is code-signed
