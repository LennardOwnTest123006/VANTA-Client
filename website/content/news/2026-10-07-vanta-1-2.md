VANTA Client 1.2.0 and VANTA Launcher 1.2.0 are out, both for **Minecraft Java Edition 1.21.11** with Fabric Loader
0.19.5, Fabric API 0.141.6+1.21.11 and Java 21, as before. They are published as the GitHub Releases
[`client-v1.2.0`](https://github.com/LennardOwnTest123006/VANTA-Client/releases/tag/client-v1.2.0) and
[`launcher-v1.2.0`](https://github.com/LennardOwnTest123006/VANTA-Client/releases/tag/launcher-v1.2.0); the
[Download page](https://vanta-client.netlify.app/download) lists every file with its size and SHA-256 once the
release workflow has published it.

## Why some of you saw exactly 30 FPS

Several reports after 1.1.0 said the same thing: *I chose Low, or the Performance profile, to get more frames, and the
game dropped to 30 FPS.* We found the cause, and it was not the HUD. In 1.1.0 the Performance Center's **Low** preset
wrote a **60 FPS cap** into Minecraft's *Max framerate* option and **Balanced** a **120 FPS cap**, and the built-in
profiles carried those caps along: the **Performance** profile (Low) a 60 FPS cap, **Default** (Balanced) a 120 FPS
cap. With a graphics driver that forces VSync at 60 Hz on top of Minecraft's own limiter, the two limiters together
can land at exactly **30 FPS**.

In 1.2.0, **presets and profiles never write a frame-rate cap.** A preset only tunes render work (render distance,
particles, clouds, smooth lighting, …); the limit lives in one place, the frame-rate limit chooser, and in the choice a
profile carries. The *Frame rate limit* row in *Settings → Performance* now really applies to the vanilla options as
well. One thing the update cannot do: undo a cap that 1.1.0 already wrote into your `options.txt`. If you applied a
1.1.0 preset or profile, choose **Unlimited** in the chooser once, or press **Boost FPS**. The
[troubleshooting guide](https://vanta-client.netlify.app/documentation/troubleshooting#low-fps-or-exactly-30-fps-after-choosing-a-preset-or-profile-in-vanta-110)
walks through it, including the driver-side VSync check.

## What VANTA itself costs, measured

To rule the HUD in or out, the headless game test that runs on every commit now measures 300 rendered frames each with
the VANTA HUD on, off and with the GUI hidden, in the real game, on a software renderer without a GPU, and writes the
numbers to `vanta-perf-probe.json`. The test fails when the **VANTA HUD averages more than 4 ms per frame**, and it
passes. That is a bound on a CI machine, not a measurement of your PC, but a cap of 60 or 120 FPS is what turns into
30 FPS, not a HUD. Along the way we cut VANTA's drawing work anyway: the main menu's horizon,
vignette and PLAY gradients are single native gradients instead of thousands of one-pixel fills, icons are drawn from a
cached set of rectangles, text widths are cached, and the HUD reads only what its enabled widgets show. The pixels are
identical; the tests say so.

## Boost FPS and Max FPS

The Performance Center has a new first preset, **Max FPS**: Fast graphics, 5 chunks, minimal particles, clouds off,
smooth lighting and entity shadows off, no mipmaps, no menu blur. Above the presets sits one button, **Boost FPS**: it
applies Max FPS, sets the frame-rate limit to *Unlimited* and VSync *off*, switches the VANTA menu to a solid background
without particles, and installs the [Performance pack](https://vanta-client.netlify.app/documentation/mods-and-shaders#the-performance-pack)
when a member is neither loaded nor installed. That last step is the only one that downloads anything, and only when you
press the button. Everything Boost does is a vanilla option or a VANTA setting you can change back by hand.

## The Performance pack in the mods bundle

For a manual installation, `vanta-client-1.2.0-mods.zip` now holds more than the VANTA jar and Fabric API: its `mods/`
folder carries the newest Minecraft 1.21.11 Fabric builds of **Sodium, Lithium, FerriteCore, ImmediatelyFast and
Iris**, unmodified as published on Modrinth and verified by size and SHA-512 when the release is built. The zip also
contains `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt` with every licence text, `PERFORMANCE-PACK.txt` with the versions in that zip (the
same list is printed into the release notes) and `performance-pack.json`. Copy **all** jars, and delete older copies of
these mods first: Fabric refuses to start with two copies of one mod, and Iris requires exactly the Sodium build next
to it.

**EntityCulling is the exception.** Its licence does not allow redistribution, so it is not in the zip. The game offers
it instead: a manually installed game asks once per game start, at the main menu, *Boost your FPS?*, and installs the
missing members from Modrinth when you say yes (*Not now* stops the question for good). Nothing is downloaded without
that click.

A word on licences: these mods are independent projects by their own authors. Sodium is licensed under **PolyForm
Shield 1.0.0**, whose terms we pass on in `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt`; the others are under MIT, LGPL and similar
licences, all reproduced there. VANTA Client itself stays MIT. The versions are not fixed in the repository: the release
workflow resolves them live and refuses to build a release when a licence text cannot be fetched or a bundled Iris
would need a Sodium other than the newest one.

## Buttons you could not click in a small window

In a windowed game (854 x 480 at GUI scale 2), at the 320 x 240 minimum or with large text, several VANTA buttons were
drawn but did nothing: *Install*, *View on Modrinth*, *Disable* and *Remove* on the Mods & Shaders detail panel, *Quit
Game* and the quick-access row of the main menu at 640 x 360, *Vanilla options* in Settings and the HUD editor's grid
and snap switches. Their panel had run out of room, so the click never reached them; maximised, everything worked. The
detail text now scrolls above a footer that keeps the actions on screen, the main menu, the Settings footer and the HUD
editor toolbar rearrange themselves to fit, and the interface never shrinks below Minecraft's 320 x 240. Every screen is
now checked at 19 window sizes and two scales for buttons that cannot be clicked, and the game test clicks the Mods &
Shaders buttons in a windowed game the way a mouse does.

## Launcher: a notification that swallowed clicks

VANTA Launcher 1.2.0 fixes one bug. In a small launcher window (960 x 600) a notification in the bottom-right corner
could lie over the lower *Install* buttons of the Mods page, and because a notification stayed open as long as the mouse
rested on it, every click there did nothing. A click on a notification now dismisses it, the hover pause ends after 8
seconds at the latest, notifications are at most 30 percent of the window wide, and in windows narrower than 1100 px,
or whenever the shown page is taller than the window and scrolls, they move to the top-right corner, below the banner
and the page header. The launcher keeps installing the whole
Performance pack from Modrinth, as in 1.1.0.

## What was not measured

- FPS gains on real graphics cards. The frame-cost probe runs on CI's software renderer and only bounds what the VANTA
  HUD costs there; how much faster your game runs without the caps, with Max FPS or with the Performance pack depends
  on your computer, and we promise no number.
- Other mod combinations: CI loads the six Performance pack mods in one game test, nothing else.
- Still true from 1.1: nothing is code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn, and Microsoft sign-in inside the
  launcher still needs a Mojang-approved client id, so PLAY goes via the official Minecraft Launcher.

## Updating

The VANTA Launcher offers both updates itself (launcher 1.0.0 still picks the file by system only; portable and jar
users there choose *Not now* and download their file from the
[`launcher-v1.2.0`](https://github.com/LennardOwnTest123006/VANTA-Client/releases/tag/launcher-v1.2.0) release).
Manual installations copy all jars from the new mods bundle and remove older copies of the pack mods first; the
[installation guide](https://vanta-client.netlify.app/documentation/installation#updating) has the steps. Compare the
SHA-256 of anything you download by hand.

As always: VANTA contains no cheats, no combat automation, no packet manipulation and no anti-cheat bypasses.
